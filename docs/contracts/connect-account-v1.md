This contract defines Connect accounts: account creation, account devices and their sign-in, the device link with safety words, vault stores (one [relay-sync-v1](relay-sync-v1.md) team for each vault), recovery, the entitlement token for other Connect services, and the internal routes of the hosted bouncer brain. Billing and groups are [connect-billing-v1](connect-billing-v1.md); the byte relay is [connect-tunnel-v1](connect-tunnel-v1.md). MUST and MUST NOT below are requirements on both sides. A change to a message, a field, or a check needs a new version of this contract.

## 1. Rules

- **No account sign-in adds a device.** A new device joins an account only by a confirmation on a verified device of the account, with the safety words of section 7 and a fresh owner check (`OwnerAction::ConfirmConnectDevice`, Touch ID or the passphrase). The confirming device signs the `add` entry of the account's signed device list for the new key (section 7.5); a client accepts a device only when the list holds it, and only a list that it verified from the account's first key, which the client pinned. There is no password, no email sign-in, and no web sign-in.
- **No answer of Connect removes a device from itself.** A refused sign-in is never a removal. Only the signed-in answer `403 device_removed` (section 6) says that a device was removed, and even then the device keeps its key and its vault rows until its owner confirms "Forget This Mac" (or iPhone) on it.
- The account has no secret on Connect. Connect holds public keys (also the account's public recovery key), the signed device lists, names, ids, hashes, and the Stripe ids of [connect-billing-v1](connect-billing-v1.md). It never holds a private key, a link code, a recovery code, a passphrase, a vault key, an item key, or a value.
- The relay-sync-v1 wire does not change. A vault store is a relay-sync-v1 team; its endpoints, heads, chain, receipts, and errors are those of relay-sync-v1.
- Requests are strict: every request body rejects unknown, duplicate, and missing fields (`deny_unknown_fields`) with `400 invalid_request`. A client ignores unknown fields in a result. Enumerations reject unknown values.
- The account routes exist only when the relay runs with `APASSY_RELAY_CONNECT_ACCOUNTS=1` or `APASSY_RELAY_MODE=connect`. Without them every route of this contract answers `404 not_found`.

## 2. Encodings

The encodings of relay-sync-v1 section 2 apply: b64u without padding, lowercase hex, the P-256 public key in X9.63 uncompressed form (65 bytes, first byte `0x04`), ECDSA P-256 SHA-256 signatures in ASN.1 DER (at most 72 bytes) as b64u, times as Unix seconds, the JSON envelope `{"ok":true,"result":…}` or `{"ok":false,"error":{"code":…,"message":…}}`.

| Vault id | the UUID of `sync_meta.vault_id`, 36 lowercase characters |

## 3. Transport

- The Connect origin follows relay-sync-v1 section 3 (`https://` anywhere, `http://` on loopback only, no path). The hosted origin is `https://apassy-relay.wyderka.cc`.
- The **edge endpoints** carry no `Authorization` header (Connect refuses a bearer there with `400 invalid_request`): `POST /v1/account`, `POST /v1/account/challenge`, `POST /v1/account/token`, `POST /v1/account/devices/join`, `POST /v1/account/devices/join/cancel`, `POST /v1/account/recover`, and `GET /.well-known/apassy-connect.json`.
- Every other route of this contract needs `Authorization: Bearer <account token>` and answers `401 unauthenticated` without a valid one (also `POST /v1/account/recovery`, the new recovery code of section 9.1). A token of a revoked device is not valid: `401 unauthenticated`, from the moment of the revoke (section 7.4). A relay-sync team token on one of these routes is `403 account_required`.
- **The account token goes only to the Connect origin** (plan 0035 I-2). A client MUST NOT send it to the bouncer brain, a node, the push gateway, the site, or Stripe, and MUST NOT write it to disk or into a vault. It lives 15 minutes in memory (`Zeroizing`).
- The Worker limits by client address: `POST /v1/account` and `POST /v1/account/recover` with `ACCOUNT_LIMITER` (namespace 1006, 5 a minute); `POST /v1/account/challenge`, `POST /v1/account/token`, `POST /v1/account/devices/join`, and `POST /v1/account/devices/join/cancel` with the existing `AUTH` limiter; `POST /v1/account/list` and the ceremony routes of `/v1/account/peers/…` (`link`, `join`, `pending`, `join/{join_id}`) with the `AUTH` limiter in a budget of their own, and the reads of lists (`POST /v1/account/peers/lists`, `GET /v1/account/peers/{account_id}`) in another (each 60 a minute for each IPv4 address or IPv6 /64). A refusal is `429 rate_limited` with `Retry-After: 60`. `POST /v1/account/recovery` (section 9.1) has no limiter of the Worker; it needs an account token. Connect also limits each device on those routes of section 16: 60 requests a minute on the ceremony routes and 60 on the reads, each budget apart, then `429 rate_limited` with `Retry-After` (the seconds until its minute ends). A client honours `Retry-After` and never takes a `429` for the end of anything. On `POST /v1/account` and `POST /v1/account/recover` the Worker also sends the coarse client prefix with its HMAC (`X-Apassy-Client-Prefix`, `X-Apassy-Client-Prefix-Mac`, the scheme of `POST /v1/connect`). Connect takes a prefix only with a valid HMAC. A new account without one counts for the caps of the relay only: behind the Worker every call carries the edge secret, and the Worker proves each call it forwards. A failed recovery without one counts for the prefix `unknown` (section 13).
- A JSON request body is at most 64 KiB.

## 4. Errors

The relay-sync-v1 codes keep their meaning: `invalid_request` 400, `unauthenticated` 401, `token_expired` 401, `forbidden` 403, `join_pending` 403, `join_refused` 403, `not_found` 404, `method_not_allowed` 405, `conflict` 409, `payload_too_large` 413, `rate_limited` 429, `relay_error` 500, `busy` 503. A client never takes `401` for a removal (section 6). Connect adds:

| Status | Code | When | Contract |
| --- | --- | --- | --- |
| 403 | `device_removed` | `POST /v1/account/token` with a valid nonce and a valid signature, for a key that was a device of the account and is not live any more (revoked, or the account was deleted). Only the device itself can cause this answer | this |
| 403 | `account_required` | A route of Connect that needs an account token, called with a relay-sync team token. A token of a revoked device is `401 unauthenticated` (section 3), and a team id that is not a vault store is `404 not_found` (section 8) | this |
| 403 | `not_in_group` | A device of another account is named for a vault store, a group route, or a dial, and the two accounts share no group (or no tunnel grant) | this, billing, tunnel |
| 403 | `plan_required` | The account is not entitled for the action: a new vault store, a push to a store, the entitlement token, a dial. A checkout by a member of a group is `409 conflict` ([connect-billing-v1](connect-billing-v1.md) section 4) | this, billing, tunnel |
| 403 | `account_limit` | A count limit of section 13: devices, vault stores, nodes | this, tunnel |
| 409 | `account_id_taken` | `POST /v1/account` names an account id that an account has or ever had (also one that was deleted). Nothing is made; the device makes a new id and signs again | this |
| 409 | `seat_limit` | Seats or people below the confirmed members, or above the maximum of the plan | billing |
| 429 | `tunnel_limit` | Open streams, dials a minute, or the monthly byte budget of the account | tunnel |
| 404 | `node_unknown` | A dial or a grant names a node that does not exist | tunnel |
| 503 | `tunnel_offline` | The node exists and has no open tunnel | tunnel |
| 410 | `free_connect_ended` | `POST /v1/connect` (the free questions-only Connect of ADR 0032), its challenge, and the questions, feed, and push routes of a free Connect team, while `APASSY_RELAY_FREE_CONNECT=0` | this |
| 410 | `hosted_retired` | In `APASSY_RELAY_MODE=connect`: a retired route of section 14 | this |

A `403 plan_required` and a `403 account_required` never carry a price, a link, or a purchase hint: the iPhone shows them as "Not on Connect" ([connect-billing-v1](connect-billing-v1.md) section 10).

## 6. Account and sign-in

### `POST /v1/account` (edge)

```json
{"account_id":"acc_k7m2q4x3ca","name":"Rafal","device_name":"MacBook Pro","platform":"mac","public_key":"<b64u>","recovery_key":"<b64u>","signature":"<b64u>","statement":"<b64u>"}
```

- `signature` is over the `apassy-connect account v1` message, which covers the recovery key. `recovery_key` is the public recovery key of a recovery code that the device made (section 9).
- `account_id` is the id of the new account, made by the device (section 2): the root entry names it. `statement` is the root entry of the device list (section 7.5): the new device key's signature over the `apassy-connect device-list v1` message of version 1 with previous `-`, op `root`, its key, link `-`, `device_name` in its canonical form (section 2), and its key as `by`. A statement that does not verify, or an id of another form, is `400 invalid_request`, and nothing is made.
- **An account id is used once.** Connect keeps every account id that it ever made (`account_id_used`, written in the transaction of the creation and never deleted). An id that an account has or ever had, also after its account was deleted, is `409 account_id_taken`, and nothing is made: the device makes another id and signs again. So no new account meets the Stripe customer, the subscription, the tunnel counts, or the keys of an earlier account of that id.
- The first device is the one that creates the account: the **root** of its device list (section 7.5). Creation is free and needs no plan. Over the account caps of section 13 the answer is `503 busy` with `Retry-After: 3600`, or `429 rate_limited` for a client prefix.
- A key is a device of at most one account: the same key with another body, or a key that is or was a device otherwise, is `409 conflict` (the client does not try again with a new id). The same body again answers the same result while the device that it made is live, at any time (a retry after a lost answer, also after a restart of the client), and counts for no limit.
- The client writes the body as its **pending creation** before the first try (`connect/pending-account.json` next to the account file, mode 0600; public values only: ids, names, its public key, the public recovery key, the two signatures), sends the same body again after a lost answer until Connect answers, and repeats a pending creation of the same origin and key at its next try, with its names. It removes the pending creation when Connect answered it (an account, `409 account_id_taken`, or a refusal other than `429` and `5xx`). The recovery code stays in memory only: a creation that a restart interrupted is finished with its body, and the app says that its recovery code was never shown. It keeps a mark of that in the account file (`recovery_unseen`), and Settings › Connect shows "This account has no recovery code that you have seen. Make a new one now." with "New Recovery Code…" (section 9.1) through every refresh and restart, until a new code succeeds.
- Answer: `{"account_id":"acc_k7m2q4x3ca","device_id":"dev_m2k7q3x4ab","created_at":1790000000}`, the account id of the body. The Mac then shows the recovery code once (section 9) and signs in.

### `POST /v1/account/challenge` and `POST /v1/account/token` (edge)

`POST /v1/account/challenge` with `{"account_id":"…","public_key":"<b64u>"}` answers `{"nonce":"<b64u of 32 bytes>","expires_at":1790000060,"origin":"https://apassy-relay.wyderka.cc"}`. The nonce lives 60 s and works once. Connect keeps no state for a nonce until it is used: the nonce carries its expiry and a MAC of Connect over the account and the key (12 random bytes, the expiry as 4 bytes big-endian, 16 bytes of HMAC-SHA256 under a key of the running relay), so a challenge never ends another challenge of the same key, and any number of challenges costs Connect no memory. A restart of Connect ends every open nonce. The client stops when `origin` differs from its configured origin. For a key whose join waits, the answer is `403 join_pending`; for a key whose join was refused or expired, `403 join_refused`; a key that is or was a device of that account (live, revoked, or of a deleted account) gets a nonce; for any other key, `401 unauthenticated`.

`POST /v1/account/token` with `{"account_id":"…","public_key":"<b64u>","nonce":"<b64u>","signature":"<b64u>"}`, the signature over the `apassy-connect sign-in v1` message, answers `{"token":"apassy_act_<account id>_<64 hex>","expires_at":1790000900,"account_id":"…","device_id":"dev_…"}`. In this order:

1. A nonce that is not one of this Connect for this account and key, an expired nonce, a wrong origin, or a wrong signature: `401 unauthenticated`, one message for every cause. The nonce is not used up.
2. A key that was a device of the account and is not live any more (revoked, or the account was deleted): `403 device_removed`. Connect gives this answer only to a request that the device itself signed.
3. A nonce that was used: `401 unauthenticated`. A full table of tokens: `503 busy`, and the nonce stays unused. Else the nonce is used up with the token.

**The client never takes a refusal for a removal.** After a `401` or a `503` of `POST /v1/account/token` it takes a fresh challenge and tries again, at most three times, 1 s and then 3 s apart; it never repeats a token request with the same nonce. Only `403 device_removed` shows "Connect says this device was removed from the account", and the device then keeps its key, its account file, and its vault rows until the owner confirms "Forget This Mac" (or iPhone) on it.

### `GET /v1/account`

Answers the `AccountView`:

```json
{"account_id":"acc_…","name":"Rafal","email":null,"created_at":1790000000,"plan":{…},"devices":[{…}],"group":null,"device_list":[{…}],"recovery_key":"<b64u>","recovery_rotated_at":1790003600,"recovery_rotated_by":"dev_…"}
```

- `email` is the email of the Stripe customer, copied by Connect after `checkout.session.completed` ([connect-billing-v1](connect-billing-v1.md) section 7), else `null`. Connect sends no mail in stage 7.
- `plan` is the `PlanView` of [connect-billing-v1](connect-billing-v1.md) section 8. `group` is `null` or `{"group_id":"grp_…","kind":"family|team","role":"owner|admin|member","state":"pending|active"}`.
- `devices` lists the live devices as `DeviceView`s: `{"device_id":"dev_…","name":"MacBook Pro","platform":"mac","public_key":"<b64u>","created_at":1790000000,"last_seen_at":1790002800}`. `last_seen_at` is rounded down to the hour, or `null`. Whether a listed device is a device of the account is the device list's to say, not this list's.
- `device_list` is the signed device list of the account from version 1 (section 7.5).
- `recovery_key` is the public recovery key that Connect holds for the account (section 2): the `previous` of a new recovery code (section 9.1). It is public, as every value of the view.
- `recovery_rotated_at` and `recovery_rotated_by` are the time and the device id of the last new recovery code that a device made (section 9.1, `account.recovery_rotated_by` next to the audit row), or `null` while the key is the one of the creation or of the last recovery.

The Mac polls this route every 3 s while the checkout sheet is open ([connect-billing-v1](connect-billing-v1.md) section 4).

### `DELETE /v1/account`

From a live device after an owner check on it (`OwnerAction::DeleteConnectAccount`), with an empty JSON body `{}`. Connect cancels the subscription at once ([connect-billing-v1](connect-billing-v1.md) section 9), deletes every vault store, device, link, grant, and node of the account, records I-4 `forget` (section 11), and keeps the Stripe customer for the invoices. Answer: `{"account_id":"acc_…","deleted_stores":2,"subscription_canceled":true}`. An organizer or a Team owner with confirmed members is refused with `409 conflict` until the members are removed.

## 7. Account devices

### 7.1 The existing device: "Add a device…"

1. The device makes a link code (32 random bytes and the link fingerprint of its pinned root key and its own key, section 2) and keeps it in memory. `POST /v1/account/devices/link` with `{"link_hash":"<b64u>"}` answers `{"link_id":7,"expires_at":1790000600}`. The link works once and for 10 minutes. The device shows the code as text with a Copy button and as a QR code of the same text (error correction M). Connect never sees the code.
2. While the sheet is open the device asks `GET /v1/account/devices/pending` every 5 s: `[{"join_id":4,"link_id":7,"device_name":"iPhone","platform":"iphone","public_key":"<b64u>","signature":"<b64u>","created_at":1790000100,"expires_at":1790000600}]`, the joins of links that this device made; `signature` is the joining key's signature of the join (7.2). For each it checks that `signature` verifies with `public_key` over the `apassy-connect join v1` message of its own link with `device_name` (a join that does not verify is not shown: Connect cannot name a device otherwise than the device named itself), computes the safety words (7.3) with the code it holds, and shows: `"iPhone" asks to join your Apassy Connect account. Both devices show: audio coyote forest mint magnet apple. Confirm only if the other device shows the same words.`
3. Confirm runs the owner check for `OwnerAction::ConfirmConnectDevice`, reads the account and verifies its device list (section 7.5), and then `POST /v1/account/devices/pending/{join_id}/confirm` with `{"public_key":"<b64u>","version":3,"statement":"<b64u>"}`: the key for which it computed the words, and the `add` entry of the device list that it signs for that key, the link hash of its link, and the device name of the pending join as the sheet showed it (the name that the join signed; the device checks the signature again before it signs), at the version after the head that it verified, over that head's list hash. Connect verifies the entry with the `device_name` of the pending join as it answers it. A list that is nearly full answers `403 account_limit` (7.5). Connect checks the entry with the confirming device's key and its own head (`400 invalid_request` when it does not verify; `409 conflict` when `version` is not the head's version and 1: the device reads the list again and signs once more), adds exactly that key with the entry, both in one transaction; a pending join whose key differs is `409 conflict`; each refusal adds nothing. Only the device that made the link may confirm or refuse (else `403 forbidden`). Answer: the new device's `DeviceView`. The confirming device pins the entry it signed as its new head.
4. Refuse: `POST /v1/account/devices/pending/{join_id}/refuse` with `{}` answers `{"join_id":4}`.

### 7.2 The new device

1. It reads the code (paste or QR), takes the account id from it, makes its account device key, and computes the link hash.
2. `POST /v1/account/devices/join` (edge) with `{"account_id":"…","link_hash":"<b64u>","device_name":"iPhone","platform":"iphone","public_key":"<b64u>","signature":"<b64u>"}`, the signature over the `apassy-connect join v1` message, which names `device_name` in its canonical form (section 2; a signature over another name, or a name that is not canonical, is `400 invalid_request`). Connect keeps the signature with the join and answers it in the pending joins (7.1). Answer: `{"join_id":4,"account_id":"…","confirmer":{"device_id":"dev_…","name":"MacBook Pro","public_key":"<b64u>"},"expires_at":1790000600,"device_list":[…]}`. An unknown, used, or expired link: `403 forbidden`. The same body again answers the same confirmer and the list as it is then.
3. It checks the confirmer: the link fingerprint of the root of `device_list` (the key of its version 1) and of `confirmer.public_key` must be the fingerprint in the code, the list must verify from that root (section 7.5), and the confirmer must be a device of it. Any other key, root, or list ends the join: the device cancels it and says that the code does not match the device that answered. It pins that root and that list, computes the words with the confirmer's key, and shows: `Waiting for MacBook Pro… Both devices show: audio coyote forest mint magnet apple.`
4. Every 5 s it asks `POST /v1/account/challenge` for its key: `403 join_pending` waits, `403 join_refused` ends, a nonce means confirmed: it signs in and reads `GET /v1/account`. It pins only a list that verifies from the root of step 3, goes on from the list of step 3, and holds its own `add`, signed by the confirmer of step 3 with the link hash of its code and the device name that it asked for; any other list ends the join here, and no account file is written. A read that fails on the way waits for the next poll.
5. Cancel before the confirmation: `POST /v1/account/devices/join/cancel` (edge) with `{"account_id":"…","link_hash":"<b64u>","public_key":"<b64u>","signature":"<b64u>"}`, the signature of the joining key over the `apassy-connect join-cancel v1` message (`400 invalid_request` without a valid one), answers `{"join_id":4}`.
6. The client keeps the **pending join** on disk from the join answer until the join ends (`connect/pending-join.json` next to the account file, mode 0600; public values only: the ids, the device name, the keys of the device, the confirmer and the root, the link hash, the version and list hash of the list of step 3, and the safety words; never the link code). It ends when the join is confirmed (the account file is written), refused or expired (`403 join_refused`, or `401` at the challenge for a join that Connect no longer has), cancelled at Connect, refused by the device (a confirmer, a root, or a list that the checks of steps 3 and 4 refuse, also at a resumed poll), or when the key was confirmed and then removed again (`403 device_removed` at the token), and when this device makes or recovers an account (it cancels the join first). When the step ends without a confirmation (the owner leaves it, a lock, a quit), the client cancels the join, best effort. When Connect does not take that cancel (it cannot be reached, or the other device confirmed first), the file stays, and the next visit of the step, also after a restart, resumes the join with its words and polls at once, so a late confirmation still ends with this device in the account. A new join of the same device cancels a pending join first. When that join was confirmed after all (its cancel came too late) and the new join then makes a new key (step 7), the device first removes its own key from the account of the earlier join, with a `remove` entry of that key signed by itself over the list that it verifies from the root that the earlier join pinned (7.4, as "Forget" does), so that account keeps no device whose key is gone; when it cannot, the join says that the other account still lists this device under its name there, and to remove it on the device that confirmed it.
7. **A key that is used.** When the join answers `409 conflict` (the key is or was a device of an account) and the device has no account file, the device asks for a challenge with that key. If it gets one, it signs in and reads `GET /v1/account`; when the device list verifies from its version 1, the link fingerprint of the code names that root with one of the live devices of the list, and the list holds the device's own key as a device, the device pins that list with the name that the list signed for its key and writes its account file, as at step 4 (the next poll does it; no words are shown, and no second device is added). Otherwise (no challenge, or a list without its key) the key is no device of that account: the device makes a new key, joins with it, and says that it made a new key and that a device of the account can remove the earlier one if the account lists it.

### 7.3 Safety words

Both devices compute

```
SHA-256("apassy-connect safety v1" 0x00 <account id> 0x00 <link code> 0x00 <confirmer public key, 65 raw bytes> 0x00 <joiner public key, 65 raw bytes>)
```

and show `WORDS[digest[0]]` to `WORDS[digest[5]]`, six words of the relay-sync-v1 list (section 16) with one space between them, the six-word form of the ADR 0032 handoff (relay-questions-v1). A typed comparison ignores case and surrounding spaces. The words cover both keys and the code. Connect never knows the code, so it cannot compute the words or make a key whose words match. The code names the confirming device's key (the link fingerprint), so the new device accepts no other confirmer key; a party that has the code (a leaked QR) can change only the joining key, and needs words that equal the words of the person's own new device: one in 2^48 for each key it tries, within the 10 minutes of the link.

### 7.4 Revoke

`DELETE /v1/account/devices/{device_id}` from any live device of the account, after an owner check on that device (`OwnerAction::RevokeConnectDevice`; a device may revoke itself, which is "Forget this device"), with `{"version":4,"statement":"<b64u>"}`: the `remove` entry of the device list for the device's key, signed by the calling device at the version after the head that it verified, over that head's list hash (section 7.5). A client signs it only for a key that the list that it verified holds as a device: it takes the key of the row, shows in the owner check the name that the list signed for that key and its key fingerprint (section 2), and refuses (nothing is sent) a row that its list does not hold, a row whose key is the client's own while its id is not, and the reverse. Connect checks it as the `add` of 7.1 (`409 conflict` for another version, `404 not_found` for a key that the list does not hold, `400 invalid_request` for an entry that does not verify; nothing changes). Answer: `{"device_id":"dev_…","removed_from_stores":2}`. At once, in one transaction with the entry: the device is revoked, its open links end, its node goes, Connect records I-4 `revoke` (section 11), and it leaves every vault store: its store rows go, so its store sign-in is refused from this moment, with a waiting removal for each store file. Then its account token and its relay-sync team tokens end, its node's tunnel closes, and each store file that is open revokes its row; a store whose file is not open (suspended, or a failed open) revokes it when it opens next, and the maintenance pass retries a removal until it is done. The last live device can be revoked, also by itself and at the last version of the list (7.5); the account then needs recovery (section 9). Its entries stay in the device list, so the devices it confirmed stay devices (section 7.5).

### 7.5 The signed device list

The device list of an account is a signed list with a cryptographic root. Connect keeps it and answers it from version 1 in `GET /v1/account` and in the join answer:

```json
"device_list":[
  {"version":1,"op":"root","key":"<b64u>","link_hash":null,"name":"MacBook Pro","by":"<b64u>","signature":"<b64u>"},
  {"version":2,"op":"add","key":"<b64u>","link_hash":"<b64u>","name":"iPhone","by":"<b64u>","signature":"<b64u>"},
  {"version":3,"op":"remove","key":"<b64u>","link_hash":null,"name":null,"by":"<b64u>","signature":"<b64u>"}
]
```

- Version 1 is the root: the first device of the account (after a recovery, the recovering device), with `key` and `by` its key, no link, its device name, and its own signature (the `statement` of section 6 or 9). Its authority is the pin of each device; its signature binds its name.
- Each later version is one change: an `add` (the confirmation of 7.1, with the link hash and the device name of the join) or a `remove` (the revoke of 7.4, also of the signing device itself). `by` signed the `apassy-connect device-list v1` message of the entry (section 5), whose `previous` is the list hash of the entry before. So each entry names the whole history before it, and Connect cannot reorder, drop, or insert an entry without breaking the signatures after it.
- A list verifies from a root when: version 1 is that root, signed by itself over its name; the versions follow each other from 1; each later entry is signed by a device of the list at the version before; an `add` names a link hash, a name (section 2), and a key that the list never held (a key that left is never added again: Connect answers `409 conflict`), and comes while fewer than 10 devices are live (section 13; a client refuses a list with more); a `remove` names a key that is a device of the list and no name; an entry of section 16 follows the rules of 16.1. The devices of the list at a version are the keys added and not removed up to it; the name of a device is the name of its `root` or `add`. At most 1024 entries. An entry that adds something (`add`, `peer`, `share`, `join`, `store`) above version 822 is `403 account_limit`: 1024 entries less the 10 live devices of section 13, the 64 peers, and the 128 shares and joins of section 16, so every live device (the last one by itself), every peer, and every share and join can still be ended up to version 1024 (a `store` never needs an end); then a recovery starts a new list (section 9). Connect refuses an entry that would break these rules.

Each client keeps in its account file the root key that it pinned when it made the account (its own key), recovered it (its own key), or joined it (the root that the link fingerprint named); the version and the list hash of the newest list that it verified (its **pin**); the devices of that list; and the keys that were devices of it and left (also those that left before this client joined). It never takes another root from Connect. A list counts on a client only when it verifies from the pinned root, is not older than the pin, holds the pinned entry at the pinned version, and holds the client's own key as a device: an older list (a rollback), another history (a fork), or a list without the client's own key counts for nothing, and the client keeps what it had. A new device pins only a list that holds its own `add` by the confirming device that its code named (7.2 step 4). The client then moves its pin to the head of the list, and a device that left the list moves to the keys that left. A device is **verified** when the list that counted holds its key. An unverified device is no device of the account for any decision of the client: it is shown as "Not verified" in Settings › Connect and on the iPhone, it is never added to a vault store, its heads are not accepted in a vault store, and it is never taken as the confirming device of a join. A device that a device of the list confirmed stays a device when its confirmer leaves later: its `add` precedes the `remove`, also on a client that joins after the removal. A client signs an entry only after the head that it verified, and pins the entry that it signed.

**The signers of a vault store** come from the trust store of the client alone, never from an answer of Connect (a group, a store marked `shared`, a device that Connect lists in the store). The current head of a vault store is accepted only when its signer's key is a device of the list that counted, or is a key that left that list (a key that it removed: it was a device of the account), and the head's version is not after the store's anchor: the version that the client had merged of that store when it learned of the removal. Each head of the chain below a head that a device of the list signed is accepted when its signer's key is a device of the list or a key that left it: the device that signed the head above it had taken it. A head of a key that was never a device of the list is refused everywhere. A live device whose own anchor head is signed by a key that left pushes a head of its own over it at its next sync, with the same content; a client whose current head is signed by a key that left, after its anchor (a device that was behind, or a new device without an anchor), waits for it and says "Waiting for another device of this account to sync", and changes nothing on its own. When no live device signs over that head (the device that left pushed last, or every other device is behind too), the owner ends the wait on the waiting device, behind a fresh owner check that names the store, that head, and the choice (`OwnerAction::TakeDepartedCopy`), with one of two actions that name the device that left by its signed name and the time of its head: "Use the Copy That <device> Left…" accepts exactly that one head once as the anchor (only for a key that the verified list removed; never for a key that the list never held, which stays refused), and the device then pushes a head of its own over it; or, on a device that has a copy of the vault, "Replace It with This Mac's Copy…" pushes that copy over it. The same owner check guards "Use the Copy That <device> Left…" on the start-screen path of a device without a vault ("Use a vault from another Mac" › "Apassy Connect"); with no vault unlocked it is Touch ID, or system authentication on Linux (no vault holds a passphrase to check), and its proof is bound to the session of that step. The step takes only the head that the check named; another head is refused, and nothing changes. The check says that the copy comes from a device that was removed from the account, names that device by its signed name, and says not to use the copy if that device was lost or stolen: "Ask on another device of the account instead, or replace the copy from a device you trust." The time of the head comes from that device's own signed head. A device without an anchor (a new device) checks the chain below that head as at any first download of a vault store: each entry that the relay serves links to the one before it and ends at the head, and each is signed by a key that the verified list holds or held; any other entry refuses the copy. It can check only the entries that the relay serves: a served chain that does not start at version 1 refuses the copy, except a chain of the head alone when it is the head that the owner's check took. After the head is taken, a later head of the same key waits again. After either, sync runs as usual. So a store is not stuck on a head that such a device pushed before it left: a live device signs over it at its next sync, or the owner's action on the waiting device does. A head or a chain entry whose signer the client does not know yet (a device that joined after the client's last list) makes it say "Checking the device list" and read the account again (within a minute); only when a list that verified on the client, and that it asked of Connect after the first refusal of that head, still does not hold the signer is the copy refused: a chain entry as a refused copy ("Use the relay copy…"), the current head as a damaged copy. A list that did not verify, a list asked before the refusal, and the end of any other step end nothing. While Connect cannot be reached the store keeps saying "Checking the device list"; after ten minutes it says that Apassy Connect cannot be reached to check the device list and that the check goes on, with "Try Again" (a new read of the account and a new sync). Meanwhile nothing is merged or pushed for that store, and the device's own changes stay in its vault: nothing is lost, and they go up with the first sync that takes the copy (when the check ends, or after "Use the relay copy…", which keeps them). "Use the relay copy…" of a vault store of a Connect account (a copy that this device refuses: older than its anchor, or a history without its last change) asks for a fresh owner check (`OwnerAction::UseRelayCopy`) that names the store and the current head; its sheet names the copy (its version, the signed name of the device that signed it, and the time of that head) and says that this device's record of what it had merged is replaced by that version. Only the head that the check named is taken; another head is refused, and nothing changes. A relay-sync team of another kind keeps the plain step. A join or an adoption, which verified the list just before, refuses such a head at once. For a store shared between accounts the lists of the other accounts are read too, and the quiet time of section 16.6 applies. Connect refuses the store sign-in of a removed device from the commit of the removal (7.4); a running sync on another device takes the removal into its signers with the next list that it verifies: the Mac reads the account at the unlock, every 30 minutes, and within a minute after a sync of a vault store was refused or waits for a device, and its sync of a store does not wait for Connect when the account file is enough (its trust store). The cost: a device from which Connect withheld a removal still takes a later push of the device that left, and its own next head carries that push into a head that the other devices take; the removal reaches it with the next list that it verifies. A device of another account (a member of the same family or team) signs only in a store that the signed `share` entries of the account that made it name for that account, through a root that the client pinned with the six words of section 16 (16.6); in every other store its heads are refused, also in a store that Connect marks `shared`.

A recovery starts a new root and a new list. The devices of the old root were revoked before it (section 9); one of them comes back only by joining again with a link of the recovered device, whose fingerprint names the new root. Connect never moves a client to another root.

## 8. Vault stores

A vault store is a relay-sync-v1 team of kind `account` with `TeamLimits::account(plan)`: `sync_bytes` 64 MiB, 10 devices for each member, no items, no agents, no questions. Its relay-sync endpoints work unchanged with team sign-in `apassy-relay sign-in v1` and the store's team id; the device key is the account device key.

- `POST /v1/account/vaults` with `{"vault_id":"<uuid>","name":"Personal","team_id":"t_…","version":5,"statement":"<b64u>"}` makes the store with the calling device as store device 1 and answers the `VaultStoreView`: `{"team_id":"t_…","vault_id":"…","name":"Personal","created_at":1790000000,"shared":false,"devices":[{"store_device_id":1,"device_id":"dev_…","account_id":"acc_…"}],"void":[]}`. `team_id` is the id of the new store, made by the calling device; `version` and `statement` are its `store` entry (section 16.1: "this account made the store `team_id`"), signed at the version after the head that the device verified. In this order: the shape (`400`); the same vault id of the same account answers the existing store (nothing is written, the entry is not appended); an entitled plan (`403 plan_required`) and the store limit (`403 account_limit`); a store id that a team has or that a `store` entry of any list names (`409 conflict`); then, in the transaction that makes the store with that id, the checks of section 7.5 for the entry (`409 conflict` for another version, `403 account_limit` for no room, `401` for a signer that is not live) and its signature (`400`). The entry, the store, and its first device row are written together or not at all. A client makes a new id for each try, and after a `409` it reads the account again and signs again; when the answer names another store than its id (the vault id had a store), it reads the list again instead of pinning its entry.
- `GET /v1/account/vaults` lists the stores that hold a device of this account, as `VaultStoreView`s. `void` lists the accounts whose active `share` of the store (in the list of the account that made it) Connect counts as void (section 16.3): the device of that account ends each such share (16.7); nothing starts from it.
- A vault store has no device link of relay-sync-v1 section 7: its relay routes other than its sign-in, `GET /v1/me`, `GET /v1/devices`, and the sync routes answer `403 forbidden`. A device of the account gets the copy of a store that lists it: it signs in to the store with the account key, downloads the head, and makes the vault with the passphrase of the copy; a device in the store adds it first (below).
- `POST /v1/account/vaults/{team_id}/devices` with `{"device_id":"dev_…","public_key":"<b64u>"}`, from a device that is already in the store, adds a live device of the account that made the store, or of an account that the list of that account shares the store with (an active `share`, section 16) and that is an active member of the same group with it (the store becomes `shared`), and answers `{"store_device_id":2,"device_id":"dev_…","account_id":"acc_…"}`. `public_key` is the key that the calling client verified (section 7.5); a device with another key is `409 conflict` and nothing is added. Any other device (unknown, revoked, of an account outside the group, or of an account that no active `share` names): `403 not_in_group`. The added device still needs the vault passphrase and merges by relay-sync-v1 sections 6 and 7. A device of another account signs in to the store only while the owner's list shares the store with its account (section 16.3, the share condition), its own list has joined the store after that share (`join`, section 16.2 step 5: the owner may add the devices before, they sign in after it), and both accounts are active members of one group. Connect checks the device, the share, and the group again in the transaction that adds it: a device that was revoked, a share that ended, or an account that left the group in the meantime is `403 not_in_group`, and nothing stays. An Apassy client adds only devices that a list it verified holds: the devices of its own account, or of an account that its own `share` names, with the root that it pinned (section 16); a device of the owner adds the verified devices of a member whose join counts and that the store does not hold yet at its refresh, without an owner check (the share covers that account's verified devices). It takes a head of another account's device only as section 16.6 says, and opens a store that Connect marks `shared` only with a share of it in its account file (else "This vault is shared with another account, and this device has not verified the share yet."), before any request; a device of a member that the store does not hold yet says "This device is not in the shared copy of this vault yet. It is added when the person who shares the vault opens Apassy on a device that syncs it."
- `DELETE /v1/account/vaults/{team_id}`, from a device in the store of the account that made it, deletes the store with its snapshots (an owner check on the Mac) and answers `{"team_id":"t_…"}`. Another caller: `403 forbidden`.
- The relay-sync challenge of a vault store keeps no state until its nonce is used: the form of section 6 with the store id in the place of the account id, still b64u of 32 bytes (relay-sync-v1 does not change). A challenge never ends another one of the same key, a used nonce is refused, and at most 30 used nonces of one key (`429 rate_limited`) and 1000 of one store (`503 busy`) are kept, each with the nonce unused.
- On both `{team_id}` routes a team id that names no vault store (no team, or a relay-sync team of another kind) is `404 not_found`.
- A push to a store of an account that is not entitled answers `403 plan_required` at the place of today's `sync_bytes` check (relay-sync-v1 section 4 order); pulls and downloads follow the plan state of [connect-billing-v1](connect-billing-v1.md) section 8. A push to a shared store (section 16) by a device of another account needs the store owner's entitled plan and an entitled plan of the pushing account: a member beyond the paid seats, or one whose plan is read-only, gets `403 plan_required` for a push and still pulls and downloads.

## 9. Recovery

The account creation makes a recovery code on the device (16 random bytes) and sends only its public recovery key (section 2). The Mac shows the code once as 16 words with "Save it in another password manager"; it is never stored by Apassy and never sent anywhere.

`POST /v1/account/recover` (edge) with

```json
{"account_id":"acc_…","device_name":"MacBook Air","platform":"mac","public_key":"<b64u>","recovery_key":"<b64u>","signature":"<b64u>","recovery_signature":"<b64u>","statement":"<b64u>"}
```

works only when the account has no live device. The message is `apassy-connect recover v1` (origin, account id, the new device's public key, the new recovery key `recovery_key` of a new code). `signature` is the new device key's signature over it (`400 invalid_request` without a valid one), `recovery_signature` the signature of the account's stored recovery key (Ed25519, 64 bytes), `statement` the root entry of the new device list (section 7.5), signed by the new device key over `device_name` (`400 invalid_request` without a valid one, checked with `signature`). Connect verifies it with the stored public key before any limit (a valid one is never limited), makes the new device the first device and the root of a new device list (section 7.5), deletes every vault store of the account (a new device list cannot verify the old heads), replaces the recovery key, keeps the subscription, and answers `{"account_id":"acc_…","device_id":"dev_…","deleted_stores":2}`. A wrong signature, an unknown account, or an account with a live device: `403 forbidden`, one message for all; a wrong recovery signature and an unknown account have the same limits (section 13) and get the same answers. The same body again within 10 minutes, while the device it made is live, answers the same result (a retry after a lost answer). Limits: section 13.

### 9.1 A new recovery code

A verified live device can replace the account's recovery code, after an owner check on that device (`OwnerAction::NewRecoveryCode`). The device makes a new recovery code (16 random bytes), shows it once as at the creation (16 words, with "Save it in another password manager"), and sends only its public recovery key. `POST /v1/account/recovery` with the account token and

```json
{"previous":"<b64u>","recovery_key":"<b64u>","signature":"<b64u>"}
```

`previous` is the recovery key that Connect holds now (`recovery_key` of `GET /v1/account`), `recovery_key` the key of the new code, and `signature` the calling device key's signature over the `apassy-connect recovery-rotate v1` message (origin, account id, the device's public key, `previous`, `recovery_key`). In this order:

1. A key that is not b64u of 32 bytes, or a signature that does not verify with the calling device's key over that message for this Connect: `400 invalid_request`.
2. A stored key that already is `recovery_key` (a retry after a lost answer): the same result, and nothing changes.
3. Else, in one transaction: a stored key that is not `previous` is `409 conflict`, and nothing changes (the device reads the account again and signs once more); a caller that is not a live device any more is `401 unauthenticated`, as any token of a revoked device (section 3); else Connect replaces the stored key with `recovery_key` and writes an audit row (section 12).

Answer: `{"account_id":"acc_…","rotated_at":1790003600,"recovery_key":"<b64u>"}`, the time at which Connect took the stored key and the key that it holds now (the `recovery_key` of the request when it was taken). From then on only the new code recovers the account (section 9): a recovery signed with the old code is `403 forbidden`, as any wrong recovery signature.

**The client.** Before the request the device writes the **pending rotation** to its account file: `previous`, the new public recovery key, and the signed statement (never the code or its private key), with the mark of a code that nobody saw. The code stays in memory. A lost answer, a `429`, or a `5xx` sends the same body again, up to four more times (Connect answers the same key again with the same result, step 2). The device reports a new code only when Connect names the new key as the one it holds (the answer, or else `GET /v1/account`); then it shows the code once, and pins that key in its account file. An answer that names another key is no new code: the device reads the account, drops the pending rotation, says so, and the code that the account had stays the one that works. A `409 conflict` reads the account and signs again; any other refusal drops the pending rotation. Without an answer after the repeats the pending rotation stays, and the next read of the account settles it, also after a restart: when Connect holds the new key, the account has a code that nobody saw (Settings › Connect keeps the line "This account has no recovery code that you have seen" with "New Recovery Code…"); when it holds another key, the pending rotation goes and the old code stays valid. The mark of a code that nobody saw goes only when the owner closes the sheet that shows the code ("I Saved It"), never with an answer of Connect: a lock or a quit before it leaves the line. When the account file cannot be written after Connect took the code, the sheet still shows the code and says that this device could not record it; the next read records it.

**Who sees a change.** Each device pins the recovery key that it saw last (its own code, or the key of the account when it joined or when its owner took note of a change; a file without a pin pins the key of the next read). Any live device may make a new code; at each read of the account, another key than the pin shows a lasting line on every other device: "The recovery code was changed on <device> on <date>. The old code no longer works.", with the signed name of `recovery_rotated_by` (or "another device") and the day of `recovery_rotated_at`, and the buttons "OK", which pins the key that the line named, and "New Recovery Code…". The iPhone core answers the same fact in `connect_status` (ios-core-v1 section 5.14).

A device that has no code that its owner saw (a creation finished after a restart, section 6, or a new code whose sheet did not close) keeps saying so until a new recovery code succeeds and its sheet is closed; the client keeps that mark in its account file.

## 10. The entitlement token

`GET /v1/account/entitlement` answers `{"token":"<payload b64u>.<signature b64u>","expires_at":1790086400}` for an entitled account, else `403 plan_required`.

- The payload is the UTF-8 JSON of the claims, compact: `{"v":1,"kid":"ek_…","acc":"acc_…","plan":"personal|family|team","group":null|"grp_…","feats":["brain","sync","tunnel"],"iat":…,"exp":…}`. `feats` is sorted, without repeats, from `brain`, `sync`, `tunnel`; v1 gives every entitled account all three. `exp` is `iat` + 86400.
- The signature is Ed25519 over `"apassy-connect entitlement v1" 0x00 <payload b64u>` with the Connect signing key (`APASSY_CONNECT_SIGNING_KEY_FILE`, a PKCS#8 file, `ring`).
- `GET /.well-known/apassy-connect.json` (edge) publishes the keys: `{"v":1,"origin":"https://apassy-relay.wyderka.cc","keys":[{"kid":"ek_…","alg":"Ed25519","public_key":"<b64u of 32 bytes>"}]}`. It lists only keys of the configuration: the signing key and the public keys of `APASSY_CONNECT_PUBLISHED_KEYS_FILE` (the next key before a rotation, the previous key for 24 hours after it). A key that leaves that file is dropped at the next start. A signing key signs tokens only after this Connect has published it for one hour, unless it is the first key this Connect ever had; before that `GET /v1/account/entitlement` is `503 busy`. So a rotation adds the new key before tokens use it, and the operator removes the old key 24 hours after the last token signed with it.
- A service checks: two parts, canonical b64u; a known `kid`; the signature; strict claims (unknown fields refused); `v` is 1; `now < exp`; `iat <= now + 300`; `exp - iat <= 86400`; its feature in `feats`; and `acc` equals the account that the service authenticated by other means (the brain: the I-3 answer for the signed-in device). It never calls Connect for a request (ADR 0035 section 5). A plan that ends is refused within 24 hours; I-4 `forget` is the hard removal.
- The token is not a credential for Connect: Connect never accepts it in `Authorization`.
- The Mac sends to the brain only `Authorization: Bearer <the brain's own token>` (from the `apassy-bouncer sign-in v1` sign-in), never the account token (I-2) and no entitlement header. The brain learns the account and its entitlement from the I-3 directory answer at that sign-in ([bouncer-brain-v2](bouncer-brain-v2.md)), and its token lives 15 minutes, so an ended plan is refused at the next sign-in. Other services check the entitlement token as above.

## 11. The bouncer brain (plan 0035 I-1 to I-5)

Status: this version of Connect records the I-4 events (`brain_event`) and serves none of the internal routes. The internal listener, the I-3 route, and the delivery of the I-4 events are a later unit; until it ships, Connect sends nothing to the brain and answers no request of it. The table is the contract of that unit.

| Item | Here |
| --- | --- |
| I-1 device key | The account device key of section 5; `key_id` is the account device id. The brain signs in with `apassy-bouncer sign-in v1`, account id in the place of the team id (vector `brain_sign_in`, section 15). |
| I-2 token | Section 3: the account token stays with the Connect client; the brain issues its own token. |
| I-3 directory | `POST /internal/v1/brain/device` with `{"key_id":"dev_…","public_key_sha256":"<64 hex>"}` answers `{"account":"acc_…","entitled_hosting":true,"entitled_training":true,"revoked":false}`. `public_key_sha256` is the hex SHA-256 of the 65 raw key bytes. `account` is the account id, opaque to the brain. Both entitlements are `"brain" in feats` of the account now (the training consent stays with the brain, J1). A revoked device answers `revoked: true` with both entitlements `false`; an unknown key id, or a hash that differs from the device's key, is `404 not_found`. |
| I-4 events | The routes of the brain: `POST /internal/v1/revoke` with `{"key_id":"dev_…"}` when a device is revoked, and `POST /internal/v1/forget` with `{"account":"acc_…"}` when the plan is deleted (90 days, [connect-billing-v1](connect-billing-v1.md) section 8) or the account is deleted. Both are idempotent. Connect records each event in the directory (`brain_event`) in the transaction of the revoke or the deletion, and prunes it 90 days after its delivery (`done_at`, set at the 2xx); an event that was never delivered stays. The later unit delivers each recorded event until a 2xx; with I-3 the brain also learns a revoke at the next sign-in of the device (`revoked: true`). |
| I-5 origin | The brain is `https://brain.apassy.wyderka.cc` (its own certificate and compose project), not behind the byte relay. The app has the origin as a build constant. |

The internal routes are served only on the internal listener of the Connect host (`APASSY_CONNECT_INTERNAL_LISTEN`, the compose network), never through the Worker, with `Authorization: Bearer <service token>` from the compose secret file `APASSY_CONNECT_BRAIN_TOKEN_FILE`; another caller gets `401 unauthenticated`.

## 12. What Connect stores

In directory 1006 (C1): `account` (id, name, created_at, public recovery key, the time of the last new recovery code of section 9.1, the creation's body hash, the last recovery's body hash and answer, email copy, group), `account_id_used` (every account id that Connect ever made, never deleted: an id is used once), `account_device` (id, account, name, platform, public key, the confirming device, created_at, last_seen hour, revoked_at; a revoked row is pruned after 90 days), `account_list` (the signed device list: each entry with its device name, its signature, and its list hash; an entry of section 16 with the other account, the pinned root or the answered share, and the store), `account_peer_link` and `account_peer_join` (the hashes of share codes, the creating device, the member, expiry, use, and the signed answers; pruned a day after they end), `account_share_void` (for an owner and another account: the owner's list version at the last removal of either of them from its group, or at an undone confirmation) and `account_join_share` (for a member and a store: the version of the owner's `share` that its last `join` answered), both dropped with the list at a recovery, `account_share_void` with either account at its deletion and `account_join_share` with its store, `account_key` (the hash of every key that was a device, with its account), `account_link` (link hash, creating device, expiry, use; pruned a day after it ends, with its joins), pending joins (key, name, platform, link), pending removals of a device from a vault store file that was not open, `vault_store` (a `team` row of kind `account` with `team.account_id`, vault id, name), the billing rows of [connect-billing-v1](connect-billing-v1.md), `tunnel_grant` and nodes of [connect-tunnel-v1](connect-tunnel-v1.md), `entitlement_key` (kids and public keys, when each was first published and last used; a record, never the source of the published keys), and the I-4 outbox (pruned 90 days after delivery). In `APASSY_RELAY_MODE=connect` these files are plain SQLite: nothing in them is secret. The snapshots in the stores are SQLCipher ciphertext under the user's passphrase.

Never stored: tokens (memory only), nonces (a used nonce is kept in memory until it expires), link codes, share codes, recovery codes, the signing key (a file), client addresses and prefixes (memory only, for rate limits), the requests of a device to the routes of section 16 (memory only, for its rate limit), vault content.

## 14. Modes and retired routes

- **Transition** (`APASSY_RELAY_CONNECT_ACCOUNTS=1`): the routes of the three Connect contracts are added to a running relay. With `APASSY_RELAY_FREE_CONNECT=0`, `POST /v1/connect` and `POST /v1/connect/challenge` answer `410 free_connect_ended` before their body is read, and so do the questions, feed, and push routes of a free Connect team (after its token, before its body); its sign-in, devices, leaving, and relay sync stay. With `APASSY_RELAY_CONNECT_SYNC_BYTES=0` as well, a push or a receipt of a free Connect team is `403 plan_required` whatever its own `sync_bytes`, and its pulls work. - **Connect only** (`APASSY_RELAY_MODE=connect`): no master key (`APASSY_RELAY_KEY` and its file form are refused at start), no push settings, no proxy. These routes answer `410 hosted_retired` before any token or body is read: `POST /v1/connect` and its challenge, `POST /v1/teams`, `POST /v1/join`, `/v1/questions` and below, `/v1/feed` and below, `/v1/push/token`, `/v1/push/handle`, `/mcp`, `/p/` and below, and every other relay route that is not relay-sync-v1 of a vault store (its sign-in, `GET /v1/me`, `GET /v1/devices`, and the sync routes). ## 16. Vaults shared between accounts

A vault store of one account syncs with the devices of another account of the same family or team only through entries of the signed device list of the account that made the store. **The account that made a store is the authority for it:** who may sign its heads comes from that account's signed entries alone. No role in a group carries trust: the group owner need not own the store, and the group view of Connect, the `shared` flag of a store (section 8), and the device rows of a store are hints only. A group admin can remove a member at Connect (connect-billing-v1 section 6), which ends that member's sign-in to the stores of the group: a second line, never a grant.

Which account made a store is in the signed lists: the device that makes a store signs the `store` entry of it in the step that makes it (section 8), and Connect appends it in that transaction. A `share` of a store counts only in a list that holds its `store` entry: every device of the account that made it and every member checks it in that account's verified list. A list that holds `store S` never joins S, so no device of that account takes S as another account's store, also a device that does not sync S. A store made before the entry existed gets it from the next refresh of a device of that account that syncs it as its own (no owner check: it adds no signer), and its shares count from then on.

### 16.1 The entries

The entries of this section are entries of the device list of section 7.5: the same `version` and `previous` sequence as `root`, `add`, and `remove`, so they have one order with the device changes, and a device that a `remove` ended signs none of them. Each is signed by a device of the list at the version before over

```
"apassy-connect share-list v1" 0x00 origin 0x00 account 0x00 version 0x00 previous 0x00 op 0x00 peer-account-id 0x00 root-b64u-or-"-" 0x00 store-id-or-"-" 0x00 by-b64u
```

On the wire an entry is `{"version","op","key":null,"link_hash":null,"name":null,"by","signature","account_id","root","team_id"}`; `root` only on a `peer`, `team_id` only on the four store ops (each absent or `null` otherwise). A client and Connect take an entry only when it follows these rules at its point of the list:

| Op | Names | Allowed when |
| --- | --- | --- |
| `store` | store S (no account, no root) | this list holds no `store S`, and S is in no active `join` of this list (Connect checks that this account made S) |
| `peer` | P, its root R | P is another account and not a peer of this list; at most 64 active peers |
| `unpeer` | P | P is a peer, and no active `share` or `join` names P |
| `share` | P, store S | P is a peer; S is in no active `join` of this list; `(S, P)` is not active. A device signs it, and Connect takes it, only when this list holds `store S`; a `share` before `store S` (a list made before the entry existed) verifies and counts for nothing until the list holds it |
| `join` | P, store S, and in the root field the list hash h of the `share S <this account>` entry of P's list that it answers | P is a peer; this list holds no `store S`, no active `share` of S, and no active `join` of S with another account; an active join of `(S, P)` that names another share is replaced by it (one active join for each pair); the same share is not joined twice. Connect takes it only when h is P's active share of S with this account. A `join` without h (a list made before) verifies and counts for nothing |
| `unshare` | P, store S | `(S, P)` is an active `share` |
| `leave` | P, store S | `(S, P)` is an active `join`; it ends the share that the join named |

At most 128 active shares and joins together. An entry that adds something (`peer`, `share`, `join`, `store`, and `add`) comes at version 822 at most (section 7.5), so every device, peer, share, and join can be ended before the list is full; a `store` never needs an end.

**A join counts** only while the share that it names is active: in P's verified list, `(S, this account)` has an active `share` whose list hash is the one that the join names, P's list holds `store S`, and Connect counts that share as not void (16.4). After P's `unshare` the join is over without a `leave`; a new `share` has a new hash, so it is a new offer, and the member joins it with a new `join`. Every device that verified the same lists computes the same.

### 16.2 The ceremony: one pin for each pair of accounts

A `peer` pins the root of another account. It is made once for each pair of accounts, with six words; later shares between the two accounts, in either direction, need no words. A (the account that shares) and X (a member of A's group):

1. A1, the vault's "Share with…": the person picks a member (the group view of Connect is only a list to pick from). A1 makes the share code `apassy_shr_<acc_A>_<64 hex>_<fp>`, `fp` the share fingerprint of A's pinned root `R_A` and A1's key `K_A1` (section 2), keeps it in memory, and `POST /v1/account/peers/link` with `{"link_hash":"<b64u>","account_id":"acc_X"}` answers `{"link_id":7,"expires_at":1790000600}` (600 s, one use, at most 3 open or used-but-unexpired codes of one device; a member outside the group is `403 not_in_group`). Connect checks the 600 s and the one use; A1 only shows the time left. A1 shows the code and its QR code: "Send this to <name>." A1 makes a code also for a member whose root this account pinned already while that member's verified list has no `peer` of this account (the person did not finish the words): the ceremony runs again, and A1 signs nothing that its list holds already (no second `peer`, no second `share`).
2. X1, Settings › Connect › "Accept a Shared Vault…": the code is pasted. X1 signs `"apassy-connect peer-join v1" 0x00 origin 0x00 acc_A 0x00 link-hash 0x00 acc_X 0x00 R_X b64u 0x00 K_X1 b64u` and sends `POST /v1/account/peers/join` with `{"account_id":"acc_A","link_hash":"<b64u>","signature":"<b64u>"}`. Connect checks the signature with X1's key and X's root (the key of version 1 of X's list), finds the unused, unexpired code of a live device of A made for X (else `403 forbidden`), uses it, and answers `{"join_id":4,"inviter":{"account_id","device_id","name","public_key"},"expires_at":…,"device_list":[…A's list…]}`; the same body again answers the same. X1 checks that the share fingerprint of version 1 of the returned list and of `inviter.public_key` is the `fp` of the code, that the list verifies from that root, and that the inviter is a device of it; else "The code does not match the account that answered.", X1 withdraws the answer (`DELETE /v1/account/peers/join/{join_id}`), and nothing is signed.
3. A1 asks `GET /v1/account/peers/pending` every 5 s: `[{"join_id","link_id","account_id","name","public_key","signature","created_at","expires_at","device_list"}]`, the answers to its codes with X's list. A1 verifies X's list from its version 1, that the answering key is a device of it, and the answer's signature. Both devices compute the six words of `SHA-256("apassy-connect share-safety v1" 0x00 code 0x00 acc_A 0x00 R_A 0x00 K_A1 0x00 acc_X 0x00 R_X 0x00 K_X1)` (keys as 65 raw bytes), shown as section 7.3 does. The store is not in the words: the pin is for the pair, and each signed `share` names its store. A1 shows: `"<name>" (<device>) asks to share vaults with you. Confirm only if <name>'s screen shows the same words.`
4. A1 "Confirm" runs a fresh owner check (`OwnerAction::ShareConnectVault`), signs `peer X R_X` and `share X S`, pins X's list in the account file, and adds X's verified devices to S (`POST /v1/account/vaults/{team_id}/devices`). When A's list pins another root for X (X was reset), the owner check is a re-pin of its own (`OwnerAction::RepinConnectPeer`, 16.7) instead.
5. X1 "The Words Match" runs a fresh owner check (`OwnerAction::AcceptConnectShare`, or the re-pin of 16.7 when X's list pins another root for A) and signs `peer A R_A`. The active shares of A's list with X of a store that A's list made then show on X's devices: "A vault shared by <name>: Join" (with "Apassy Connect calls it "<store name>"." as a hint, or the name of the vault on that computer when one syncs it), except a store that X's own list made, shares, or joins from another account, or that a vault of that computer syncs as its own, except a share that X's join names already, and except a share that X left (X's list has a `leave` of the join that named it: the same share is not joined again, a new `share` is). A newer share of a store that X's list joined from A (A ended the share and shared it again) is offered too. Join runs a fresh owner check (`OwnerAction::JoinConnectShare`), reads A's list again (the share must be active, name X, and not be void), signs `join A S h` naming that share, and reads the lists of the co-members of S before the first download; the copy is downloaded and opened with the vault's passphrase ("Use a Vault from Another Mac" › Apassy Connect), or the vault of that computer that synced S syncs it again.

Each owner check of this section names what is signed: the vault by its name on that computer (else the name that Connect gives the store, for a store of this account; Join names a vault that no vault of that computer syncs as "a vault shared by" the person, with Connect's name of it as a hint only), the person by the device name that the root of their verified list signed with the short fingerprint of the pinned root (the name that Connect gives the person shows beside it as a hint only), the step: `ShareConnectVault` (share, with the words or without them for a pinned pair), `AcceptConnectShare` (accept), `RepinConnectPeer` (a new root, with the shares and joins that end first), `JoinConnectShare` (join), `EndConnectShare` (stop sharing or leave), and the exact entries that the step signs, in their order (their ops, accounts, and stores). The device reads its own list just before it builds the check and again before it signs; it signs exactly those entries or nothing: a list that needs other entries now (another device pinned another root meanwhile) makes the proof fail, and nothing is signed. The proof covers exactly those fields. A share of a store whose `store` entry the own list does not hold is refused before any check.

Words that differ, or a code that expired: nothing is signed, and the person makes a new code. Connect never knows the code: it cannot compute the words, and a party with the code (a leaked QR) can change only the answering key, which gives other words. `GET /v1/account/peers/join/{join_id}` answers `{"join_id","state","expires_at"}` (`pending`, `confirmed` once A signed its `peer` of X, `refused`, `cancelled`, `expired`) to the answering device and the device that made the code; `DELETE` refuses (the maker) or withdraws (the answerer) a pending answer. Second devices of either person read the lists (16.6) and need no ceremony.

### 16.3 `POST /v1/account/list`

`{"version":5,"op":"share","account_id":"acc_…","root":null,"team_id":"t_…","statement":"<b64u>"}` appends one entry of 16.1, signed by the calling device (`account_id` is `null` exactly for a `store`; `root` is the root of a `peer` or the answered share of a `join`). Before it stores the entry, Connect checks, in this order: the shape (`400 invalid_request`: an op of section 7.5, this account, a root field other than on a `peer` or a `join`, a store other than on a store op); for a `peer`, `share`, or `join`, that both accounts are active members of one group (`403 not_in_group`); for a `share`, that the plans of both allow sharing, an entitled Family or Team plan (`403 plan_required`); then in one transaction the group and the plans again, read on that transaction (a removal from the group or an end of a plan that commits first refuses the entry), the version (`409 conflict`) and the room (`403 account_limit`) of section 7.5, the signer (`401` for a device that is not live in the list), the signature (`400`), the rules of 16.1 (`409 conflict`; `403 account_limit` over the counts), and the conditions of each op: a `store` names a store that this account made (`403 forbidden`; `404` for no store); a `peer` names the key of version 1 of P's list (`409 conflict`); a `share` names a store that this account made (`403 forbidden`; `404` for no store) and whose `store` this list holds (`409 conflict`); a `join` names a store that P made, whose `store` P's list holds, and the hash of the `share` of it with this account that P's list holds active by the share condition below (`409 conflict`). An `unshare` removes P's devices from S, and a `leave` this account's devices from S, in the same transaction (section 7.4, the two halves of a removal).

The share condition of Connect, for a device of P in a store S of this account: this list holds `store S`; the last `share` or `unshare` of `(S, P)` in this list is a `share`; this list's last `peer` of P names the key of version 1 of P's list now (after P's recovery the old pin adds and signs in none of P's new devices until this account signs `unshare`, `unpeer`, `peer` of the new root, and `share`); the `share` is newer than the last void mark of the pair (a removal of P or of this account from its group, and an undone confirmation of either, voids at Connect every `share` that names the removed account and every `share` that it made, in the transaction of the removal; only a newer `share` lifts it: **a removal is never a grant**); and both accounts are active members of one group. A device of P is added to S under it; it signs in to S only while, besides, P's list has an active `join` of S that names that `share` (after a `leave`, or a newer `share`, P signs `join` again). Answer: `{"version":5,"op":"share"}`. The client pins the entry it signed.

### 16.4 The lists of other accounts: `POST /v1/account/peers/lists` and `GET /v1/account/peers/{account_id}`

A caller may read the list of another account when both are active members of one group, or when a vault store that exists has both as participants: the account that made it and every account that its list names in a `share` of it (also one that ended). Connect decides it from the stored signed entries; it ends when the store is deleted. So a device that was offline while a member wrote to a store, and the member left the group, still reads that member's keys. Every other caller is `403 not_in_group` (`400` for the caller's own account).

`GET /v1/account/peers/{account_id}` answers `{"account_id","name","devices":[{"device_id","name","platform","public_key"}],"device_list":[…],"void":[{"team_id","account_id"}]}`. The list is that account's whole list: its readers see its peers (accounts and pinned roots), the stores it made, and the ids of the stores that it shares and joins, with the accounts they name. Which of the rows is a device of that account is its list's to say; the client verifies the list from the root that it pinned and adds only those devices to a store. `void` names the shares of that list that are active in it and that Connect counts as void (16.3); a client takes them only to end that share here (16.6, 16.7), never to start anything.

A refresh reads the lists in batches: `POST /v1/account/peers/lists` with `{"accounts":[{"account_id":"acc_…","version":12,"head":"<list hash>"}]}`, 1 to 64 distinct accounts other than the caller, each with the pin of its list that the client holds (`0` and `""` for none). The answer has one item for each asked account, in the asked order: `{"account_id","state":"newer","name","device_list":[…],"void":[…]}` when the head of that list differs from the asked pin (newer, or another history: the client decides), `{"account_id","state":"same","void":[…]}` when the head is the asked pin, `{"account_id","state":"refused"}` for an account that the caller may not read (or none), and `{"account_id","state":"later"}` for a list that did not fit (the lists of one answer are at most 1 MiB together; the first newer list always comes). The reads count in a budget of their own (section 13). A client reads 64 accounts at a time, starts its order at another account at each refresh, waits the `Retry-After` of a `429`, and never takes a `429`, an error, or `refused` for the end of anything: an account that it did not read keeps what it had.

### 16.5 The account file

Version 4 of the account file adds (a file of version 3 moves in place, and no device joins again: it is read as version 4 without peers and shared stores and written as version 4 at its next change; a file of version 3 with a field that version 3 does not have is refused, as is a file over 4 MiB):

- `"peers"`: the pins, one for each pair of an account and the list that named its root, `{"account_id","root","via","name","list_version","list_head","known","left","suspended"}`, unique by `account_id` and `via`. `via` is `null` for a root that this account's own `peer` pinned with the words (at most 64: the active peers of its list), or the account `acc_…` whose verified list named this root in its `peer` entry, for a co-member of a store of that account (at most 256). A root named by one account counts only in that account's stores: two owners that name different roots for one account have two pins, each used in its own owner's stores. `name` is the device name that the root signed in version 1 of that account's list (empty before the first list), the verified name that the owner checks show. `list_version` and `list_head` are the pin of that account's list (0 and empty before the first list); `known` (at most 16) its devices at the pin; `left` (at most 32, the oldest first out; never a live key) its keys that left; `suspended` that Connect gave a list of another root. A pin's list pin is never reset while its root stays: a list older than the pin, or a fork of it, is not taken. When the naming list pins another root (after a reset and new words), the pin starts again from the new root, and its old devices and keys that left stay as keys that left. A pin goes when no list and no store needs it.
- `"stores"`: at most 192 of `{"team_id","owner","members","ended"}`: `owner` is `"self"` or the account that made the store, `members` the accounts of the owner's active `share` entries for it (of a store whose `store` the owner's list holds), `ended` (at most 64, the oldest end first out) the accounts whose share of it ended in the owner's verified list (an `unshare` and no active `share` since). Both come from the owner's verified list, so every device that verified the same list has the same members and ends, also a device that reads a `share` and its `unshare` at once. A store of another account whose join does not count here (16.1) keeps no members and no ends: it has ended here. A store that this account's list left (no active `join`) goes from the file, with the pins that only it needed. A file of the first build of version 4, whose `ended` held keys, drops those entries when it is read and is written back.
- At the cap of 256 pins through owners, the pins that no store needs any more go before a new co-member is refused (the stores of a refresh are set before any new pin is made). A pin's list pin is never reset while its root stays and a store uses it; a pin that no store and no list needs goes, and starts again from no list when it comes back.

### 16.6 Which keys sign the heads of a shared store

From the account file alone, never from an answer of Connect:

- A store S that this account joined (its own list has `join A S`): A's list is verified from the own pin of A; M is the set of accounts that A's list has an active `share` of S with (S made by A: A's list holds `store S`). When the join does not count (16.1: this account is not in M, or the join names another share than A's active one, or Connect counts that share as void), S has ended here, and no key signs a new version. Otherwise the accepted keys are A's devices, this account's devices, and the devices of each P in M through the pin of P whose `via` is A (the root that A's list names for P; this account's own pin of P does not count in A's stores).
- A store S of this account: M is the set of accounts that its own list shares S with; the accepted keys are its own devices and the devices of each P in M through its own pin of P.
- The keys of a suspended pin are not accepted. The keys that left (this account's, A's, each member's), every key of an account in `ended` (its devices and its keys that left, through the pin of that store's owner), and the devices of a suspended pin sign a head only at or below the anchor of S, and a head of the chain below an accepted head. A key in none of these lists signs nothing.

Without an active share nothing changes from section 7.5. When a verified list drops P from M, or P's list removes a key, those keys sign as ended keys or keys that left: heads merged before stay, later ones are refused, and the re-push of section 7.5 over a head of a key that left applies. The signers of each store are made from the account file after every step that writes it (a step of the ceremony, a share, a join, an end, a verified list), and reach the sync that runs at once, whether or not a call to Connect worked. A device reads its own list and the lists of its peers, of the owners of the stores it joined, and of their co-members (also of the accounts whose share ended: their keys sign only as ended keys) at each refresh of the account (16.4), and a Join reads the lists of the co-members before the first download. Every device that verified the same lists computes the same signers. A head of a shared store whose signer (or the signer of a head of its chain) is no key of these lists is the sync state "checking the device list" of section 7.5, never a damaged copy at once, also in the download of a join (its session follows the signers of the account file from its first request): the device reads the lists of the other accounts again at once, after 30 s, and then after twice the last wait, up to 10 minutes, with no registration of devices, and Settings shows "Checking who shares this vault…" and no error for 10 minutes, once for each head (a lock does not start it again). Only lists asked of Connect after the refusal of that head, after those 10 minutes, decide it: the lists of every account of the store (its owner, its members, its ended accounts) and the own list, each read and verified in one refresh (a read that failed, was refused, or did not verify counts for nothing, and so does a read asked before the refusal that answers after it). When they still do not hold the signer, the copy is refused as section 7.5 says (a chain entry as a refused copy, the current head as a damaged copy), and the error shows. While no such read comes (Connect cannot be reached), the store keeps checking and never says damaged; after 10 minutes of checking its line says that Apassy Connect cannot be reached to check the device list, with "Try Again", as for a store of one account (7.5). The window hands the signers of the account file to the sync together with the time of the newest list that verified in them, as one value. A head of a key that left, after the anchor, says "Waiting for a device that shares this vault"; "Use the Copy That <device> of <person> Left…" (section 7.5) takes such a head of an ended key of another account by the signers of that store, and names the device and the person by their verified lists.

### 16.7 The end of a share, and a reset account

The owner's "Stop Sharing with <name>…" signs `unshare` and the member's "Leave This Shared Vault…" signs `leave`, each after a fresh owner check (`OwnerAction::EndConnectShare`). Leave turns off the sync of that vault on the leaving device and keeps the vault there; the member's other devices turn it off when they read the `leave` ("You left this shared vault on <device>."). The lines of the section come from the lists, so they say the same after a restart: a member's row "<name> stopped sharing this vault." while the owner's list has no active share that its join names, an owner's row "<name> left this vault." or "Sharing with <name> ended." with "Share with <name> Again…". A device of the owner signs `unshare` by itself, without an owner check, in three cases only, and at most one entry for each share (a share that another device ended already is not ended again):

- the member's verified list left the share that is active now: it has a `leave` of a join that named that share ("<name> left this vault.");
- the group answer of Connect shows this account as an active member of its group, and does not show the member or names it as removed ("Sharing with <name> ended: no longer in your family or team."; the row shows the verified name, never an account id). A `404` or a `405`, an error, an empty answer, an answer without this account, or one where this account is not active ends nothing;
- the store view of Connect marks the share void (16.3; the same note).

An end gives no one access: a lying Connect can end a share this way, never start one. `unshare`, `leave`, and `unpeer` are never signed under a share, accept, or join owner check: each check names the exact entries of its step (16.2). The `peer` stays, so sharing again needs no words. A recovery of either account starts a new root (section 9): the old pin stays, the other side marks the account suspended ("<name> reset the account. Share again." on the owner's devices, "<name> must verify this account again." on a member's), and new words are needed. The device that pins the new root runs an owner check of its own (`OwnerAction::RepinConnectPeer`, with both root fingerprints, the words, and the vaults whose shares and joins end), checks before the first entry that the list has room for the whole sequence (the `peer` and a `share` after it are adding entries, 7.5), and signs the `unshare` and `leave` entries of the old root, `unpeer`, `peer` of the new root, and on the owner's side `share` of the store of the code.

### 16.8 What a device cannot detect

- A suffix of a list that Connect withholds (a `remove` or an `unshare`), or an old prefix that Connect gives to a new device or member: the device keeps the last list that it verified, until Connect serves it a newer one. Until then it takes the heads of the keys that the withheld entries ended, and its own later head carries what it merged; the other devices take that head, and the merged heads below it (a key that ended signs below a head of an accepted key). No head carries a list, so a head of an up-to-date device does not end the share there either.
- A permanent partition: Connect can keep the devices of a share apart.
- The time between an admin's removal of a member at Connect and the next refresh of a device of the owner, which then signs `unshare` (16.7): the member's devices sign in no more, and their heads count until the owner's list ends the share.
- The old keys of an account that reset, on a device that learns that account only after the reset: Connect keeps no list of an old root, so the chain entries of the old keys after that device's anchor are refused ("Use the relay copy…"); a device that read the old list before keeps them as keys that left.
